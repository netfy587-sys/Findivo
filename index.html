Where to start

Reading time · 4 min

Most plants that don't survive their first month weren't a bad choice — they were the wrong plant for the light and attention actually available. Before buying anything, it's worth being honest about the room: how much direct sun a window really gets, how often you realistically remember to water, and whether the plant needs to survive a forgetful week now and then. This guide starts there, then moves through care basics, seasonal shifts, and the mistakes that quietly kill more plants than pests ever do.

The three basics

Get these right first

Light

Match the plant to the window, not the room. "Bright indirect" means near a window but out of direct midday sun — a few feet back usually does it.

Water

Check the soil with a finger before watering rather than following a fixed schedule — overwatering, not underwatering, kills more houseplants.

Soil & drainage

A pot without a drainage hole is one of the most common reasons roots rot — water needs somewhere to go once the soil is saturated.

Care through the year

Season by season
Spring

Growth speeds up after winter — this is the best time to repot, start feeding again, and move plants to a slightly brighter spot if they've been dormant.

Summer

Watering needs usually go up with heat, but check the soil rather than assuming — an air-conditioned room can dry out slower than expected.

Monsoon

Humidity does a lot of the work for you. Cut back watering frequency and watch for yellowing leaves, which often signal too much moisture rather than too little.

Winter

Growth slows, and so should watering. Move plants away from cold windowpanes and stop feeding until new growth appears again in spring.

Common mistakes

Worth avoiding
✕

Watering on a fixed scheduleEvery room and season changes how fast soil dries out — check with a finger before watering rather than watering "every Sunday" regardless.

✕

No drainage holeA decorative pot with no way for water to escape traps moisture around the roots, which is one of the fastest ways to cause rot.

✕

Repotting too oftenMost plants only need a slightly bigger pot every year or two — constant repotting disturbs roots more than it helps growth.

✕

Ignoring the seasonFeeding and heavy watering in winter, when most plants are dormant, does more harm than good — ease off until spring.

Choosing the right pot

Go one size up from the nursery pot, not several — too much extra soil holds water longer than the roots can use.
